## What it took to run outside the US (the minimum, all recorded as fallbacks in each manifest)

- `dem`: Copernicus DEM GLO-30 (global; NASADEM stops at 60 N, so Lofoten failed at the first step), canopy not removed.
- `naip`: a Sentinel-2 L2A cloud-masked median composite at 10 m (`fetch_global.sentinel_true_colour`).
- `hydro`: straight to OSM outside NHD (the NHD service answers empty there, not an error); its Overpass query moved to the
  osm step's reader, which falls back to curl on a TLS failure (this Mac's Python could not reach overpass-api.de).
- `landcover`: ESA WorldCover 10 m, Meta/WRI canopy height, ERA5 climate and SoilGrids written as LANDFIRE-shaped rasters
  (synthetic EVT/BpS codes per ecosystem group, EVC/EVH in LANDFIRE's own code scheme), so every reader works unchanged;
  plus the ecosystem map (`pipeline/landcover_global.py`, `pipeline/ecosystem.py`).
- `ground`: soils from SoilGrids 2.0 in SSURGO's encoding (`pipeline/soils_global.py`); geology was already global (Macrostrat).
- `plants` / `species_mix`: outside the regional tables a community is filled from the tile's own researched species that
  have a record (`species_mix.research_rule`); known species need 2 GBIF records instead of 10 on such tiles; library
  species tagged with the tile's region are candidates even when records missed them.
- 14 trait cards for the probes' dominant plants (umbrella and whistling thorn, desert date, sausage tree, red oat grass;
  downy birch, rowan, heather, crowberry, juniper; three dipterocarps and Macaranga), grown by plantgen on the closest
  library templates. No Meshy credits.
- Fixes the probes forced: a treegen crash on a generated record whose reference tree has no crown envelope (guarded);
  a race between parallel builds reading a half-written manifest (`write_json` is now atomic); the licence audit
  correctly refused the ecosystem layer until its source (IUCN GET, CC BY 4.0) was registered.

Each probe: about 27 minutes of machine time (9-13 of it the Sentinel-2 seasons, 4-5 the GBIF species), 8-22 MB counted
downloads (the cloud-optimised GeoTIFF reads of GLO-30, WorldCover, canopy and Sentinel-2 are not counted by the meter),
100-137 MB on disk, 15-20 MB for a player to enter it. No Meshy credits; the animal lists would queue 890-1,700.

## What the probes say phase 1 must be

Most of the stack carried over: terrain, water routing, land cover, canopy, geology, soil and the species research all
produced plausible layers anywhere with modest changes, and the step graph, fingerprints, cost meter and licence audit
held. What failed is where a place's **life** is decided: who grows where, what the ground is made of, which animals are
drawn, and when the seasons are. In order of effect:

1. **Composition by niche** (the largest visual and ecological error). A niche block on every plant card (elevation band,
   aspect, height above stream, wetness, rock and soil, light), filled from the card's sources and from the species' own
   occurrences sampled against the tile's rasters; abundance from cover (vegetation plots, literature, the research
   agent), not records; growth form from a trait source instead of a family guess.
2. **Animals from the tile's own list.** The pipeline writes, per researched species, an archetype, a stand-in body (an
   existing model recoloured and scaled by its traits) and its behaviour numbers; the client spawns only the tile's
   species. Calendars and needs from traits, so every species has games.
3. **The ground and the palette.** Ground materials, grass and forbs from the tile's ecosystem and species, not the
   foothills'; haze and colour calibrated against Sentinel-2, with the overlap numbers above as the metric.
4. **Water permanence from climate and drainage area**, not NHD codes; the sea surface and shore.
5. **Seasons from the tile's own climate** (wet and dry, hemisphere, daylight) in the pipeline and the client.
6. **Terrain**: national lidar outside the US through the lidar step; GLO-30 lowered by the canopy where there is none.
7. **Geology**: a per-source licence filter on Macrostrat; finer national maps where open; depth to bedrock.
8. **The satellite judge, automated** (done by hand here: render top-down, compare with Sentinel-2).

New generator forms (buttressed emergents, umbrella crowns, heath carpets, palms) are real gaps but come after 1-3: with
the right species in the right places and the right ground, the existing trees already read as a rainforest from above.

**What the four add.** The US data path works on new land, and where the North American library covers a place (the
Tetons, the Canyon) the result reads as the place from every pose: terrain, geology, cover and animals are all real. The
global path's weak points are the same as before and sharper in the tropics: an empty species mix lets the client's
California fallback draw pines in the Amazon; the humid tropics' haze washes the scene out; and the regional tables
(three regions) leave Arizona and Wyoming to California's readings. The Grand Canyon shows the geology primitive is
right and the client's rendering of rock is the bottleneck.

Additions to phase 1 from these four: **(a)** the client must never draw another region's species as a fallback (draw
the ecosystem card's functional types, or nothing, and say so); **(b)** formation colour and banding from the geology
units' descriptions (a rock look per unit, not three classes); **(c)** humidity haze calibrated against the imagery in
the humid tropics; **(d)** the regional tables replaced by the niche composition, which needs no region (until then, a
tile outside every region reads no regional lexicon).

Fixes forced on the way: a shadowed variable in the dem step's Planetary Computer fallback (it wrote the open raster into
the manifest; reached because USGS's elevation service answered 502), and Macrostrat's coverage test on coastal tiles.
Cost of the four: about $13 of Claude (with the design additions), no Meshy credits, 38-57 minutes of build each.
